Case Overview

CASE STATUS: UNRESOLVED

URAL POLYTECHNIC INSTITUTE — EXPEDITION 09 — CLASSIFICATION: RESTRICTED

Expedition Summary

In late January 1959, a group of ten experienced hikers associated with the Ural Polytechnic Institute prepared for a Category III winter expedition into the Northern Ural Mountains. The group was led by Igor Dyatlov, a 23-year-old radio engineering student known for previous successful treks.

The party consisted of eight men and two women. All members possessed significant experience with difficult terrain, extreme cold, and multi-day winter travel. The expedition was expected to last approximately three weeks and would qualify participants for higher hiking certifications.

The nine who continued after Yuri Yudin turned back: Lyudmila Dubinina, Yuri Krivonischenko, Rustem Slobodin, Aleksander Kolevatov, Semyon Zolotaryov, Yuri Doroshenko, Zinaida Kolmogorova, Igor Dyatlov, Nikolay Thibeaux-Brignolle.

MISSING PERSONS REPORT — FILED FEBRUARY 1959

Disappearance

When the group failed to return by the expected date, families contacted the Institute. Initial delays occurred due to assumptions of weather-related slowdowns. Search parties were eventually organized.

Investigators located an abandoned tent on the slope of Kholat Syakhl ("Dead Mountain"). Evidence indicated the tent had been cut open from the inside. Belongings remained inside. Tracks led away from the shelter into the freezing night.

Search party examines the tent cut open from the inside — late February 1959

INVESTIGATOR NOTE — ARCHIVE RECOVERY

Your Assignment

You have been granted access to a reconstructed digital archive containing recovered expedition records, search-party notes, photographic plates, and radio intercept fragments. Your task is to reconstruct the final days of the expedition, identify missing or anomalous evidence, and determine what the restricted materials may conceal.

Proceed methodically. Compare documents. Examine timestamps. Note inconsistencies. Some evidence will only become available after prior stages are resolved.

Later accounts and some recovered notes mention brief observations of unusual lights in the sky on the night of 1–2 February. These reports remain unverified and form part of the enduring mystery surrounding the case. The official investigation did not establish a connection.
